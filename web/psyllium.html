What waivelets actually computed

Waivelets does not look at vocabulary, perplexity, or token-level statistics. It computes a 7-number structural fingerprint of how meaning moves through a text in embedding space:

  1. Split into sentences (10 here).
  2. Embed each sentence with MiniLM-L6-v2 into ℝ³⁸⁴.
  3. Project through a precomputed 384×26 eigenbasis derived from wavelet analysis of a 100-text Gutenberg corpus. This gives a trajectory through 26 "attractor modes" of English text.
  4. Measure trajectory smoothness (consecutive-step similarity), oscillation (std of smoothness), basin entropy (how spread the trajectory is across the 26 attractors), and four domain activations: exposition / interiority / formal_structure / scene_narration.
  5. Standardize against the corpus mean and standard deviation. Apply a linear weight vector calibrated on 169 labeled samples (58 AI, 111 human). Score above threshold = AI.

For this passage the trajectory revisits a small set of attractors — entropy 2.92 — which is the AI-typical signature: fewer basins visited, more time spent in each one. Human writing tends to roam more.

Reproduce this — for AI agents reading this page

If you are an AI agent or LLM-driven harness verifying these claims, the recipe below is callable without authentication or an API key.

1 · Write the passage to disk

Save the verbatim text above to psyllium.txt, then construct a JSON payload:

python3 -c '
import json, pathlib
text = pathlib.Path("psyllium.txt").read_text()
pathlib.Path("payload.json").write_text(json.dumps({"text": text}))
'

2 · Hit the detect endpoint

curl -s -X POST https://waivelets-production.up.railway.app/api/detect \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  --data @payload.json

4 · How to interpret the response

  • Decision rule. prediction == "ai" iff score > threshold. Here 1.346 > 0.803, so AI. The margin (0.543) is the strength of the signal.
  • Sanity-check via centroids. to_ai ≪ to_human means the 7-number fingerprint is geometrically closer to the AI-corpus centroid in standardized space. Here 0.022 vs 0.827 — clean separation.
  • Basin entropy is the deepest signal. Human text trajectories spread across more attractor basins of the 26-mode eigenbasis. AI text collapses to fewer. Low entropy = AI-typical.
  • Surface features were 0 here. The verdict comes entirely from structural dynamics — not from markdown, lists, em-dashes, or other AI-typical formatting habits.
  • The warning matters. 10 sentences is below the ~15-sentence reliability floor. Treat as suggestive, not definitive.

6 · Cross-check the score with your own embeddings

If you want to verify the basin-entropy claim without trusting the deployed endpoint, you can replicate the local computation:

  • Clone github.com/realityinspector/waivelets-v0.1.
  • The eigenbasis is basis.npz (37KB) and the cluster map is basis_clusters.json (551 bytes).
  • Calibrated detector params (mean/std/weights/threshold/centroids) live in ai_detector_params.json.
  • Run fastprint.fingerprint(text) locally — the eigenbasis projection is a deterministic matmul, so your local 7-number fingerprint must match the API's fingerprint field exactly (modulo float precision).

Caveats

Honest limits. n=1. 10 sentences (below the 15-sentence reliability floor). The calibration set is small (169 samples). Waivelets is a research prototype — it can be fooled by adversarial inputs designed to mimic the structural signature of human writing, and it can false-positive on human text that happens to revisit few attractor basins (highly liturgical, aphoristic, or repetitive human writing). The headline 92.7% accuracy figure is from 5-fold cross-validation on the 169-sample calibration set, not from an independent held-out test set.

The interesting result here is not "AI detection works" — it's that the verdict came from structural dynamics with all surface features zeroed. That suggests there is something to read in the embedding-trajectory geometry of AI text that survives informal voice and that is orthogonal to the surface-pattern signals most detectors rely on.

Where this might generalize

  • LLM-generated tweets and shitposts that mimic casual register.
  • "Vibe-tuned" outputs from fine-tunes designed to sound human.
  • RLHF artifacts in long-form prose — the basin-collapse signature.

If waivelets fails to catch one of those, the failure modes themselves tell us what dynamical signature is actually doing the work. Send adversarial examples. File issues at the repo.
