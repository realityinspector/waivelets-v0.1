Experiment v0.3 · 8 models · 96 generations · 2026-05-27

Can waivelets tell which model wrote this? Yes — 88.5% of the time.

Up from 76% in the first writeup. Adding a label-aware basis trained with adversarial topic-debiasing (~48 dimensions, gradient-reversal head on prompt index) pushes the spectrometer to within 1pp of the raw-embedding upper bound (89.6%). Prompted by this question.

What we tried, ranked

All leave-one-document-out, 8-way model identification, 50–100 permutation nulls. The blue/green chance and ceiling lines bracket the range. Every condition above 50% is significant at p<0.02.

Five families of method, three of which beat the prior 76% spectrometer cleanly:

  • Adversarial topic-debiased basis (48-D) → 88.5%. Train a small linear projection W with two heads: predict model identity (maximize) AND predict prompt index (minimize via gradient reversal). Forces W to encode model voice without topic confound. Closest single feature space to the 89.6% ceiling, in 8× fewer dimensions.
  • LDA basis alone (21-D) → 85.4%. Simplest label-aware move: directly find the 7 axes that maximize between-model vs within-model variance from per-sentence MiniLM embeddings. No PyTorch, no metric learning. +9.4pp over baseline for free.
  • Wavelet-fit LLM basis stacked with Gutenberg (156-D) → 84.4%. Replaces the naive PCA-derived LLM basis with the original v0.1 wavelet methodology — continuous wavelet transform per dimension, cross-dimension correlation, null-subtracted eigendecomposition. The wavelet basis is also nearly perpendicular to the Gutenberg basis (mean |cos| 0.11) but captures multi-scale temporal coordination that PCA throws away.
  • Contrastive triplet basis (48-D) → 83.3%. Train W with standard triplet loss so same-model sentences cluster. Worse than the adversarial cousin (no topic-debiasing head), better than pure LDA (non-linear in training, even though projection is linear).
  • Per-sentence + majority vote → 53.1%. Each sentence gets independently classified; document prediction = majority vote. Informative — proves there IS per-sentence signal — but the document-level fingerprint is doing real work beyond averaging.
Headline. The single best move was making the basis itself label-aware. Cross-validated supervised projection (LDA-derived) closes most of the gap; adding adversarial debiasing of the prompt confound closes the rest.

The lesson about stacking

Concatenating bases helps when each basis sees something the others miss, AND we have enough samples to estimate the joint covariance. With 8 classes × 12 samples each (n=96), stacking past ~150 dimensions starts hurting — the LDA classifier's within-class covariance estimate gets ill-conditioned. We saw it three ways:

  • Adversarial alone (48-D) → 88.5%. Adversarial + everything else (273-D) → 79.2%. The stack adds noise faster than signal.
  • Wavelet-spectrometer (Gut+Wavelet, 156-D) → 84.4%. Adding PCA-spectrometer too (234-D) → 82.3%. Same pattern.
  • Triple Gut+LLM-PCA+LDA (177-D) → 75.0%. The LDA basis is so concentrated (21-D) that the 156-D unsupervised features just dilute it.

The right architectural reading: pick the best label-aware basis as the primary fingerprint, then add unsupervised bases as auxiliary "structural priors" only if dimensionality stays well under n. With more data (n=500+) the stacking story probably flips — that's a v0.4 experiment.

Per-model recall from the prior 76% Gut+LLM-PCA spectrometer (we haven't yet re-run the confusion matrix for the new 88.5% adversarial result — the diagonal would be tighter and the off-diagonal smaller, but the family-confusion structure should be similar):

Easiest models to identify: claude-sonnet-4 and gpt-4-turbo (11/12). Hardest at this point: gpt-5, gemini-2.5-pro, llama-3.3 — all confused with each other. The adversarial basis closes about half of that residual confusion (overall 88.5% vs 76% with the same models).

Where it gets things right and where it doesn't

The diagonal is where it gets the model right. The off-diagonal cells show which model it mistook the text for. The pattern is informative:

  • Anthropic models are easiest. sonnet-4 and gpt-4-turbo land 11/12 correct. Internal confusion within the Anthropic family is rare.
  • Within-family confusions cluster. The 3 GPT models overlap somewhat; gpt-4o gets mistaken for gpt-4-turbo, gpt-5, and even llama-3.3 once each.
  • Reasoning-trained models bleed into open-source. gpt-5, gemini-2.5-pro, and llama-3.3 confuse with each other — a likely "reasoning trace + post-training distillation" signature, or maybe just the high-temperature long-output regime.

How it works — the two-light spectrometer

The trick was to stop using one basis and use two. The Gutenberg basis (from human literary text) sees structural categories of human writing. A second basis fit on the LLM corpus sees the directions LLMs actually vary along. The two bases are nearly perpendicular — mean |cos| between best-matched eigenvectors is just 0.11 — so they pick up complementary signal.

Stacked together (156 numbers per text instead of 78), the spectrometer lifts accuracy from 58% (Gutenberg alone) and 70% (LLM alone) to 76% (both). The bases stack instead of competing.

Setup

8 models × 12 prompts ≈ 500-word generations at temperature 0.7, via OpenRouter, ~$1.50 total spend. Reasoning models (gpt-5, gemini-2.5-pro) needed max_tokens=4000 to leave room past their CoT. We also added a small subset of OpenRouter free models (glm-4.5-air, kimi-k2.6, qwen3-next, nvidia-nemotron-3-super, nvidia-nemotron-nano, deepseek-v4-flash) for a sanity-check expansion — 14 generations across 5 free models, not in the main 8-way comparison but available in the raw data.

Main models tested: claude-sonnet-4, claude-sonnet-4.5, claude-haiku-4.5, gpt-4-turbo, gpt-4o, gpt-5, gemini-2.5-pro, llama-3.3-70b-instruct. Bases saved: llm_basis.npz (PCA), llm_wavelet_basis.npz (wavelet).

Caveats

  • n = 12 per model. Enough for family signal, not enough to separate close siblings (sonnet-4 vs sonnet-4.5 still confusable).
  • Same prompts. Per-prompt content variance dominates per-model voice variance. Unsupervised clustering on the 384-D embeddings finds zero model structure — LDA only works because we hand it labels.
  • Temperature 0.7. Varied temps widen intra-model spread.
  • Reasoning traces invisible. Only final message.content is captured.
  • MiniLM-conditioned. Different embedder → different separation. The signal is partly a fingerprint of MiniLM reading the models.
