Can waivelets profile individual models? Yes — once you build a second light.

Two bases (one from human literature, one from LLM output, nearly perpendicular) stacked together → 76% model-ID, 73% family-ID on leave-one-out LDA. Single bases get half that lift. Prompted by this question.

Setup

8 models × 12 prompts ≈ 500-word generations at temperature 0.7 via OpenRouter. ~$0.85 in spend. Reasoning models (gpt-5, gemini-2.5-pro) needed max_tokens=4000 to leave room past their CoT.

Models: claude-sonnet-4, claude-sonnet-4.5, claude-haiku-4.5, gpt-4-turbo, gpt-4o, gpt-5, gemini-2.5-pro, llama-3.3-70b-instruct.

One — the basis matters more than the readout

The original 7-D fingerprint discriminates models at chance (11.6% 8-way, p=0.43). But the full 78-D readout of the same Gutenberg basis (mean projection + |mean| projection + basin histogram across 26 attractors) gets 58% model-ID. The basis carries the signal; the 7-number summary panel was throwing most of it away.

In raw 7-D space, closest centroid pairs are across families (haiku-4.5 ↔ gemini at 0.49). Within-cluster spread is twice between-centroid distance. Models look fully overlapped. That picture lies — the basis sees the difference, the summary just hides it.

Two — the bases are nearly perpendicular

We fit a 26-D PCA on every sentence from the 96 generations (3 084 sentences) → an LLM-corpus basis. Mean |cos| between each Gutenberg eigenvector and its best-matched LLM-PCA eigenvector: 0.11 (range 0.04–0.18). The two bases point in almost different directions in ℝ³⁸⁴.

That orthogonality is the load-bearing fact: it's why the two readouts add instead of overlap. Gutenberg basis alone → 58% model-ID. LLM basis alone → 70%. Both concatenated → 76%. Stacking, not redundancy.

Three — the synth metaphor and the next moves

The eigenbasis is the patch bay. The 7-number fingerprint is the dashboard speedometer. We were trying to read a tachometer signal off the speedometer. Adding a second basis is adding a module to the rack — same instrument, more cables, richer spectrum per text.

Build a third basis (technical prose, dialogue, poetry, code) and the spectrometer gets sharper. Replace the naive PCA fit with the original wavelet pipeline and the LLM basis gains structural meaning (it currently mixes topic and style). Per-model wavelet bases would let us probe training-process traces — pruning, curation, RLHF, synthetic supplementation — as nameable axes.

The new basis is at web/assets/llm_basis.npz. Drop-in compatible with basis.npz.

Caveats

  • n = 12 per model. Enough for family signal, not enough to separate close siblings (sonnet-4 vs sonnet-4.5 still confusable).
  • Same prompts. Per-prompt content variance dominates per-model voice variance. Unsupervised clustering on the 384-D embeddings finds zero model structure — LDA only works because we hand it labels.
  • Temperature 0.7. Varied temps widen intra-model spread.
  • Reasoning traces invisible. Only final message.content is captured.
  • MiniLM-conditioned. Different embedder → different separation. The signal is partly a fingerprint of MiniLM reading the models.
